What it is

A production B2B financial-intelligence platform for banks and commercial lenders. A loan officer can't answer "is this business expanding, over-leveraged, and when should I call them?" from any single data feed. The platform fuses four hard modalities onto one Report object — regulatory filings, behavioral foot traffic, official geostatistics, and unstructured web — then scores the result and recommends the banking products the business is a fit for.

It isn't a dashboard bolted onto a data vendor. It's a complete intelligence product: autonomous data assembly, deterministic scoring, a controlled product-recommendation engine, and a full cost-and-audit trail behind every report.

Who it's for

Banks & commercial lenders

Each tenant is an institution carrying a credit limit, contract dates, and weekly report and pipeline limits — a hosted SaaS sold to lending institutions.

Loan officers

Submit a business and get back a scored, defensible report — plus prospecting pipelines that auto-discover comparable businesses nearby.

Credit committees

Every grade is computed by deterministic math against config thresholds, so a recommendation is auditable and defensible — not a black box.

Data fusion no single feed delivers

Four independent modalities are resolved, scored, and unified onto one report. The value is the fusion: each source answers a different question, and only together do they describe a business well enough to lend against.

The sharpest commercial signal

UCC filings are public records of secured loans against a business's assets — they reveal who has already lent to a business and when those liens lapse. The platform turns that into engagement windows: a six-month window ending at each active lien's lapse date.

In other words, a calendar of exactly when a competitor's financing expires and the bank should call to pitch refinancing — derived automatically from multi-state public records.

AI explains, but math decides

The headline composite Score and every sub-grade are computed deterministically — averaging metrics against configured thresholds — so a grade is defensible to a credit committee. The language models never decide a number; they explain.

Free-form AI findings are funneled into a controlled business-state vocabulary (expanding vs. shrinking, stable vs. volatile cash flow, excess vs. low reserves, and so on). Those states feed a rules engine that emits per-product fit on a "highly likely → highly unlikely" scale, filtered to the products each institution is actually licensed to sell.

Why it matters: the output is auditable. Every grade traces back to numbers and thresholds, and every product recommendation traces back to a controlled state — not a sentence a model happened to generate.

How it's used

Reports

Submit one business; get a scored, ~17-section report. Reports expire after 30 days and self-renew for active subscriptions.

Pipelines

Submit one address + business type; the platform auto-discovers comparable businesses, reports on each in parallel, then ranks and caps the top results.

Watchlists

Per-institution report lists with priority and subscription status, plus admin bulk-CSV upload that spawns a report per row.

Multi-tenancy is enforced at the data layer: a user belongs to one institution and can only ever retrieve that institution's data. Report creation is metered against weekly limits, and an admin cost-analysis view meters per-report model cost across tenants.
